EEG-Based Alzheimer's Progression Prediction

Classifies resting-state EEG scalograms as Alzheimer's Disease or Healthy Control, and separately estimates brain age from the same scans, using GAN-augmented training and Grad-CAM explainability. Results below are the actual output of Run 1 — not projected or placeholder figures.

Root cause, found and fixed The negative R² traced to a real bug: the GAN augmenter stamped every synthetic scalogram with one constant age (the class mean), so in Fold 2 alone 1,299 training images carried an identical age label — teaching the regression head a distorted signal rather than just diluting it with noise. Fixed by bootstrap-resampling a real subject's age onto each synthetic image instead. A second real-data run with the fix has not yet completed as of this report.

LIMITATIONS

Known limitations, as of Run 1

AD vs. HC, not sMCI vs. pMCI — the clinical question answered is "does this EEG look like AD or a healthy control," not disease progression.
Small sample — 20 subjects means small per-fold test sets and non-trivial variance across folds (reported as mean ± std throughout).
Low-fidelity GAN — 50 epochs is short of convergence; treat the with/without-GAN comparison as the actual evidence for whether augmentation helped, not an assumption.
64×64 scalograms — discard fine-grained time-frequency detail that 224×224 would retain.
ICA fallback — uses a kurtosis-threshold heuristic when no EOG-proxy channel is usable, coarser than a dedicated EOG channel.
